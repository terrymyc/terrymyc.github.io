Research

My research focuses on improving the quality and efficiency of survey data collection. I study how recruitment decisions affect who responds and how well survey findings reflect the population. I combine statistical methods, simulation studies, and computational tools to investigate these questions.

My collaborative research extends to health behavior, survey attitudes, and how people understand and evaluate public opinion polls.

Doctoral dissertation

On Effective and Efficient Survey Data Collection at the Crossroads of Statistics and Artificial Intelligence

University of Michigan, 2026

My dissertation investigates three ways to improve survey data collection: identifying groups that may benefit from different recruitment strategies; using predicted survey answers to help estimate participation; and using AI-generated profiles to prioritize recruitment.

Manuscripts in preparation

The analyses for the three dissertation studies are complete, and manuscripts are being prepared for submission. Titles below are working titles.

In preparation

A Stratification Method for Evaluating and Improving the Representativeness of Survey Response

Develops a method for grouping potential respondents by their estimated chances of participating, helping researchers decide where to direct different data collection strategies.

In preparation

Leveraging Survey Variables to Estimate Response Propensity in Adaptive Survey Design

Examines whether predicting survey answers before estimating participation can help researchers design recruitment strategies that reduce bias in survey findings.

In preparation

Predicting Survey Participation from LLM-generated Profiles Improves Recruitment Efficiency

Investigates a recruitment approach that converts background information into textual profiles and uses active learning to prioritize outreach. The study evaluates this approach through simulation using existing survey data.
